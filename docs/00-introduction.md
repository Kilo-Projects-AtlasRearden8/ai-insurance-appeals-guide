## What is an EOB anyway?

An **Explanation of Benefits** (sometimes called an *EOB* or *EOPA*) is a
statement your insurance company sends you after they process a claim. It is
**not** a bill. It is a summary that says:

- What service was billed.
- What the insurer decided to pay (or not pay).
- What portion, if any, you owe.
- If something was denied, *why* it was denied.

You will receive an EOB for every claim your provider submits to your insurer. You
should review each one — even when your provider says "everything was covered."

## The appeal process, in plain English

When a service is denied, you usually have the right to **appeal** that decision.
An appeal is your chance to say: "I think you made a mistake, and here is why."

There are three levels of appeal in most plans:

1. **Internal appeal** — You send more information to your insurance company. They
   review it and make a new decision. This is the most common first step.
2. **External review** — If the internal appeal is denied, an independent third
   party reviews your case. This is sometimes called a *peer-to-peer review* or
   *independent medical review*.
3. **State insurance commissioner or court** — If both internal and external
   reviews are denied, you may be able to escalate to your state's insurance
   department or to court.

> **Important:** Each level has its own deadline. Missing a deadline usually
> means you lose the right to appeal that denial. Deadlines are almost always
> measured in days from the date on the EOB or the denial letter — not the date
> you open the envelope. We cover deadlines in detail in [Step 4](04-tracking-deadlines.md).

## How to use AI with this guide

AI is a **drafting assistant**, not a decision-maker. It can:

- Help you organize your thoughts before you write.
- Turn bullet points into polished paragraph form.
- Check that your letter includes the key pieces of information.

AI **cannot**:

- Tell you whether a denial was correct or incorrect.
- Guarantee that your appeal will be approved.
- Replace a lawyer, a medical professional, or your own judgment.

Treat AI output as a *first draft* that you read, edit, and verify before you
send it. We cover safe AI practices in [Step 5](05-using-ai-safely.md).
