# Step 3: Writing Your Appeal Letter

This is the heart of the process. You will draft a letter that explains why the
insurance company should reverse the denial. AI can help you organize your
thoughts and turn them into clear, professional language — but you are the final
editor.

---

## The structure of an appeal letter

Every appeal letter should include these elements, in this order:

```
1. Your contact information (return address block)
2. Date
3. Insurance company name and address
4. Subject line (clear and specific)
5. Member ID and claim number (in the body, near the top)
6. Statement of disagreement (what you are appealing)
7. Summary of the issue (the facts, in your own words)
8. Your argument (why the denial was wrong — supported by evidence)
9. List of evidence attached
10. Request (what you want them to do)
11. Signature and printed name
```

### 1. Your contact information

```
Dylan Asdale
123 Main Street
Indianapolis, IN 46201
Phone: (317) 555-0123
Email: dylan@example.com
```

### 2. Date

The date you are sending the letter. If you are mailing it, use the postmark
date. If you are submitting through an online portal, use the date you submit.

### 3. Insurance company address

Find this on your EOB or insurer's website. It is usually a claims processing
address, not corporate headquarters.

### 4. Subject line

Make it specific and easy to find:

```
RE: Appeal of Claim Denial — Claim #[number] — Member #[ID]
```

### 5. Member and claim info

```
I am writing to appeal a denial under the following claim:
  Member ID: XXXXXXXXX
  Claim Number: CLM123456789
  Date of Service: October 5, 2026
  Denial Code: CO-45 (Requested information not provided)
```

### 6. Statement of disagreement

One clear sentence:

```
I disagree with the denial of this claim and request a full review.
```

### 7. Summary of the issue

Two to four sentences describing the situation in your own words. Be factual,
not emotional:

```
On October 5, 2026, I received [service] from [provider]. My
provider submitted the claim to [insurer], which returned an EOB dated
October 20, 2026 showing a full denial under code CO-45 ("requested
information not provided"). I believe all necessary documentation was
submitted at the time of service, and I have enclosed corrected materials
for review.
```

### 8. Your argument

This is where you present your evidence and explain why the denial was
incorrect. Structure it as: "The insurer denied this because of X, but here is
why X does not apply."

#### Example argument (medical records not provided):

```
The denial code CO-45 indicates that requested information was not
provided. However, my provider's records show that all required
documentation was submitted on [date] via [method — fax, mail, portal].
I enclose:

  - The completed claim form (Page 1 of 2)
  - The medical records for the date of service
  - The prior authorization reference number [PA-987654321]

These documents were part of the original submission. I respectfully
request that the claim be reprocessed with these materials.
```

### 9. List of evidence

Use a bulleted list:

```
Enclosed:
  - Copy of the EOB (Claim CLM123456789)
  - Medical records for October 5, 2026
  - Physician statement from Dr. Smith (dated October 22, 2026)
  - Copy of prior authorization PA-987654321
  - Copy of plan document, page referencing coverage for this service
```

### 10. Request

End with a clear, specific request:

```
I request that this claim be reprocessed and that the denial be
reversed, resulting in payment of the full allowed amount less my
applicable copay or deductible. Please send confirmation of this
decision to the address above.
```

### 11. Signature

If sending by mail: sign your name in blue ink (or any color — the point is
it looks original). If submitting online, type your full name.

## How to use AI to draft your letter

AI is excellent at organizing your bullet points into polished prose. Here is
the workflow:

### Step 1: Gather your facts first

Before you open an AI tool, collect:

- Claim number
- Date of service
- Denial code and its meaning
- Total billed and patient responsibility
- What evidence you have (or plan to get)

Write these as simple bullet points. Do not rely on AI to remember details from
one session to the next.

### Step 2: Feed the facts to AI

Use a prompt like the one in `docs/prompts/appeal-letter-prompt.md`. Here is a
simplified version:

> "I need to write an insurance appeal letter. Here are the facts:
> - My name: [name]
> - Member ID: [ID]
> - Claim number: [number]
> - Date of service: [date]
> - Denial code: [code] — [meaning]
> - Denied amount: $[amount]
> - Why I think the denial was wrong: [your explanation]
> - Evidence I have: [list]
>
> Please write a complete, professional appeal letter following this structure:
> [list the 11 elements from above]. Keep the tone factual and respectful. Do
> not add information I did not provide."

### Step 3: Review and edit

**Never send AI output as-is.** Always:

- [ ] Replace any placeholder values ([name], [ID], etc.).
- [ ] Verify every fact against your actual documents.
- [ ] Remove anything that sounds emotional or accusatory.
- [ ] Make sure your signature line is your real signature (or typed name if
  online).
- [ ] Read it out loud — if it sounds robotic, simplify the language.

### Step 4: Save your version

Save the final letter as a dated PDF or Word document:

```
YYYY-MM-DD_appeal-letter_CLM123456789.pdf
```

## Common letter mistakes (and how to avoid them)

| Mistake | Why it hurts | How to fix it |
|---------|-------------|---------------|
| Forgetting the claim number | The insurer cannot match your appeal to the denial | Put it in the subject line and the first paragraph |
| Being too emotional | Makes it harder to read objectively | Stick to facts; one sentence about frustration is fine |
| Including new medical claims | Confuses the issue | Focus on the single denied service |
| Sending unrequested attachments | May cause delays | Only send what you list in your evidence section |
| Missing the deadline | Ends the appeal | Track deadlines (Step 4) |
| Sending to the wrong address | Loses time | Use the address on the EOB or denial letter |

## Sample appeal letter template

```text
[Your Name]
[Your Street Address]
[City, State ZIP]
[Phone] | [Email]

[Date]

[Insurance Company Name]
[Claims Processing Address]
[City, State ZIP]

RE: Appeal of Claim Denial — Claim #[NUMBER] — Member #[ID]

Member ID: [XXXXXXXXX]
Claim Number: [CLM123456789]
Date of Service: [Month DD, YYYY]
Denial Code: [CODE] — [Plain English meaning]

I am writing to appeal a denial under the above-referenced claim. I
disagree with the insurer's decision and request a full review of the
case.

On [date of service], I received [service description] from
[provider name]. My provider submitted the claim, and my insurer
returned an Explanation of Benefits (EOB) dated [EOB date] showing
a full denial under code [CODE].

[Your explanation — 2 to 4 paragraphs — of why the denial was wrong,
 supported by evidence you are attaching.]

Enclosed:
  - Copy of the EOB
  - [List all supporting documents]

I request that this claim be reprocessed and the denial reversed.
Please send written confirmation of your decision to the address
above.

Thank you for your time and consideration.

[Signature]
[Printed Name]
```

## Quick checklist: Before you move on

- [ ] Your letter includes: your info, date, insurer address, subject line,
  member ID, claim number, and date of service.
- [ ] You clearly state that you disagree and are requesting an appeal.
- [ ] Your argument is factual and supported by evidence.
- [ ] You list every enclosed document.
- [ ] You have reviewed and edited AI-generated text (no placeholders left).
- [ ] You have saved the final letter with a dated filename.
