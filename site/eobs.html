Step 1: Reading Your Explanation of Benefits (EOB)

Before you can appeal a denial, you need to understand what was denied and why. That information lives on your EOB. This page walks you through reading one — even if it looks like a wall of text and numbers.

What an EOB looks like

An Explanation of Benefits (sometimes called an EOB or EOPA) is a statement your insurance company sends you after they process a claim. It is not a bill. It is a summary that says:

  • What service was billed.
  • What the insurer decided to pay (or not pay).
  • What portion, if any, you owe.
  • If something was denied, why it was denied.

Section by section

1. Patient and Claim Information

FieldWhat to look for
Patient name and date of birthConfirm this matches you. If it does not, the claim went to the wrong insurer.
Subscriber/member IDYour insurance card number. Write it down.
Claim numberA unique identifier (often starts with CLM or H). You will need this on every appeal letter.
Date of serviceThe date or date range the service was provided. This must match your records.
EOB date / Received dateThe date the insurer processed the claim. Your deadline clock usually starts here.

2. Summary (also called "Payment Summary" or "Explanation")

FieldMeaning
Total billed (or "Billed charges")What the provider submitted.
Allowed amountWhat the insurer says is reasonable for this service.
Insurer-paidWhat the insurer actually paid.
Patient responsibilityWhat you owe (copay, deductible, coinsurance, or the full amount if denied).
If "Insurer-paid" is $0.00 and "Patient responsibility" matches the full billed amount, the claim was denied. This is the most common denial scenario.

3. Denial Reason Codes

Insurers use standardized codes to explain why they denied or reduced payment.

CO codes (Claims Adjustment Reason Codes / CARCs)

CodeMeaningWhat it means for you
CO-29Charges are above the plan's allowanceThe provider billed more than the negotiated rate. You may still owe coinsurance on the allowed amount.
CO-30A third party is responsibleThe insurer thinks someone else should pay. You may need to involve the other party.
CO-45Requested information not providedThe insurer needs more documentation. Gather the missing records and resubmit.
CO-48Re-submission of a claim with different revenue centersAdministrative error — ask the provider to correct and resubmit.
CO-95The benefit for this service is included in another benefitThe service is bundled into another one.

PR codes (Patient Responsibility codes / Remittance Advice Remark Codes)

CodeMeaning
PR-1Deductible not met
PR-2Coinsurance applicable
PR-3Copayment applicable
PR-62Non-covered service
PR-150Provider not in network
PR-165Not covered unless certified in advance

RP codes (Remittance Advice Remark Codes)

CodeMeaning
RP-1006Service not covered by benefit
RP-160Charges exceed the fee schedule
RP-22Documentation not sufficient to determine severity
RP-455Prior authorization required
Pro tip: Write down the exact code and its description. You will reference it in your appeal letter. Insurers often have a code lookup tool on their website.

4. Service Details Table

Each row in this table represents one service that was billed. For each service, check that:

  • The date of service matches what you (or your provider) recorded.
  • The CPT or HCPCS code matches the service you received.
  • The description makes sense for the service.
  • The billed and paid amounts look correct.

If any of these are wrong, note it down. Billing errors are grounds for appeal.

Common EOB red flags

Red flagWhat it might mean
Service dates you don't recognizeThe provider may have billed for services you didn't receive, or there is an identity mix-up.
A code you don't recognizeLook it up. Many codes have specific meanings that affect coverage.
"Patient responsibility" equals the full billed amountThe service was likely denied in full.
Multiple EOBs for the same claim numberThe claim may have been re-processed. Check each EOB.
No denial reason listedRequest a detailed explanation in writing.

Quick checklist: Before you move on

  • You have recorded the EOB received date.
  • You have recorded the claim number.
  • You have identified the denial reason code(s).
  • You have confirmed the date of service and total patient responsibility.
  • You have noted the appeal deadline.
