Step 4: Tracking Deadlines

Missing a filing deadline is the most common reason appeals fail — not because the argument was weak, but because the insurer is no longer required to consider it. This page shows you how to track deadlines so you never miss one.

Why deadlines matter

Every denial triggers one or more deadlines. Miss one, and your right to appeal that specific denial expires. The clock typically starts on the EOB date or the denial letter date — not the date you discover the denial.

Common deadline types

Deadline typeTypical lengthWhen it starts
Internal appeal180 daysEOB date or denial letter date

External review (hearing)Varies by stateDate of external review request
Renewed internal appeal60–90 daysIf you get new evidence after the first appeal

These timelines vary by state and plan. Check your plan document and the denial letter for the exact deadline. When in doubt, assume the shorter period applies.

How to find your specific deadline

1. Check the denial letter (not just the EOB)

Many plan members stop at the EOB. The denial letter — sometimes a separate document — usually states the deadline in plain text:

"You have 180 days from the date of this letter to appeal this decision."

2. Check your plan's Summary of Benefits

Look for a section called "Appeals" or "Grievances and Appeals." It will list the standard timeframes.

3. Check your state's insurance regulations

Most states require a minimum of 180 days for internal appeals. You can search "[Your state] insurance appeal deadline."

4. Call member services

Ask specifically: "What is the deadline to appeal claim [number]?" Write down the name of the representative and the date of the call.

Pro tip: If a representative gives you a different deadline than what is in writing, go with the shorter one. When you have two conflicting deadlines, use the earlier date.

How to track deadlines

You do not need expensive software. Here are three options, from simplest to most powerful.

Option A: A simple table (Google Sheets or Excel)

Create a spreadsheet with these columns:

ClaimDenial DateDeadlineDays RemainingStatusNotes
CLM123...2026-10-202027-04-1669Draft letter

Use these formulas:

  • Days Remaining: =Deadline - TODAY() (format as a number)
  • Highlight past due: Conditional formatting → cells less than today → red fill.
  • Highlight approaching: Conditional formatting → cells within 14 days → yellow fill.

Option B: A wall calendar

Mark every denial date with a red circle. Then count 180 days forward and mark the deadline with a bold X. Add a reminder 14 days before the deadline.

Option C: Digital tools

  • Google Calendar — Create an event on the deadline date, then set a reminder 14 days prior.
  • Todoist / Things / Microsoft To Do — Create a task with a due date.
  • Airtable — If you want linked records between claims, evidence, and correspondence.

What to do when a deadline is near

Days remainingAction
30+No rush, but review the evidence.
30–14Draft the appeal letter and gather documents.
14–7Schedule a final review. Send early if possible.
7–3Submit the appeal (email, portal, or certified mail).
3–0Submit immediately. If the deadline falls on a weekend or holiday, submit the business day before.
0 (past deadline)Submit anyway with a "timely good cause" explanation. See below.

What if you miss the deadline?

You are not automatically out of luck. Most insurers and states allow an appeal to be filed late if you can show good cause (also called "excusable neglect").

Good cause examples:

  • You were hospitalized or incapacitated during the deadline period.
  • You were misinformed by an insurer representative (with notes or a recording).
  • You received the EOB very late.
  • You were caring for a seriously ill family member.

What to include in a late filing:

Add a short paragraph to the top of your appeal letter:

Note on Timeliness: I am filing this appeal after the standard deadline due to
[reason]. I have enclosed [supporting documentation — doctor note, call log,
etc.]. I request that this appeal be accepted under the "good cause" exception.
Even with good cause, some insurers and states do not allow late filings for external review. File early whenever possible.

Quick checklist: Before you move on

  • You have identified the appeal deadline for every denied claim.
  • The deadline is recorded in your tracking system (spreadsheet, calendar, or app).
  • You have set a reminder at least 14 days before the deadline.
  • You have a process for tracking all correspondence.
  • You know what to do (and write) if you miss the deadline.
