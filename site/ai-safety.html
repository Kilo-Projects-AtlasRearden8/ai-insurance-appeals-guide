Step 5: Using AI Safely and Ethically

AI is a powerful drafting assistant — but it is not a doctor, a lawyer, or an insurance expert. This page explains how to use AI effectively while staying safe, truthful, and within bounds.

AI is a tool, not an authority

Never treat AI output as a final answer. Always verify:

What AI producedWhat you should do
A denial-code explanationLook it up in the insurer's official code list.
A quote from your planCheck the PDF of your plan document.
Legal terminologyConsult your state insurance department or an attorney.
A deadline calculationConfirm against the denial letter or your state regulations.
A medical necessity argumentHave your doctor review it.

Privacy: What to share (and what never to share)

NEVER share this with any AI tool:
  • Your full Social Security Number.
  • Your member ID or claim numbers (mask the middle digits).
  • Your full address or phone number (use a generic placeholder).
  • The names of other people in your household.
  • Detailed medical conditions (use codes, not descriptions, when possible).
  • Bank account or payment information.

Safe inputs:

  • The denial code (e.g., "CO-45") and its public meaning.
  • The type of service (e.g., "MRI lumbar spine without contrast").
  • General timeline facts (e.g., "EOB received October 20, service date October 5").
  • The structure of your argument (not the specific personal details).
Best practice: When using AI, replace personal details with placeholders: 
Member ID: XXXXXX[[MASKED]]
Name: [Patient]
Address: [Redacted]

Then fill in the real values yourself when you assemble the final letter.

Using local AI (no internet, no data sharing)

If you prefer to keep everything private, you can run AI models locally on your computer. No data leaves your machine.

ToolWhat it doesSkill level
LM StudioRuns many open-source models locally with a friendly GUIBeginner
OllamaCommand-line tool for downloading and running LLMs locallyIntermediate
GPT4AllDesktop app with offline modelsBeginner

What you need

  • At least 8 GB of RAM (16 GB recommended).
  • At least 4 GB of free disk space for a small model (7–15 GB for larger ones).
  • A modern CPU (an Apple Silicon Mac, AMD Ryzen, or Intel i5+ works fine).

Quick start with LM Studio

  1. Download LM Studio from lmstudio.ai.
  2. Install and open it.
  3. Browse the "Models" tab and download a lightweight model like Phi-3-mini-4k-instruct or Llama-3-8B-Instruct.
  4. In the "Chat" tab, paste your prompt and hit send.

Local models produce slightly lower-quality output than cloud models, but for drafting appeal letters, they are more than sufficient — and they never send your data anywhere.

Cloud AI: When and how

Cloud models (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini) produce higher-quality text. Use them when:

  • You have stripped all personally identifiable information (PII).
  • You are using a business or enterprise-tier account with a data-usage agreement you have read.
  • You are only using the output as a draft to edit yourself.
Never input raw medical records, full SSNs, or claim numbers into a free consumer AI chat.

How to prompt AI for appeal-writing

The quality of AI output depends heavily on the prompt. Here is a framework:

GOOD prompt:

"I need to draft an insurance appeal letter. Here are the facts: [bullet
list of facts]. The denial code is [CODE] with the meaning [description].
I have attached [list of evidence]. Please write a professional appeal
letter following this structure: [list the sections]. Keep the tone
factual and respectful. Replace all personal details with placeholders
like [Your Name]."

BAD prompt:

"Write an insurance appeal for me."

The bad prompt produces a generic template with no real facts. AI cannot fill in details you never gave it.

Common AI mistakes and how to catch them

MistakeHow to catch it
Fabricating case lawAI sometimes cites non-existent court cases. If it mentions a specific law or case, Google it or look it up yourself.
Inventing denial-code meaningsAI may misstate what a code means. Always check the insurer's official code list.
Overstating medical necessityAI may write that a service is "clearly medically necessary" without evidence. Tone down absolute claims.
Adding fake evidenceAI may list documents you do not actually have. Remove any attachment references that are not real.
Missing the deadlineAI may compute 180 days incorrectly. Double-check with a calendar.

Checklist: Before you send anything

  • I have removed or masked all PII (SSN, full address, claim numbers).
  • Every fact in the letter matches my actual documents.
  • Every cited denial code is verified against the insurer's official list.
  • The deadline date is calculated and confirmed by a calendar.
  • My physician (if involved) has reviewed the medical-necessity language.
  • I have signed the letter (physically or typed name for online submission).
  • I have saved a dated copy of everything I send.

Where to get help

If you need more support than AI can provide:

  • State insurance commissioner — Free help with disputes and appeals.
  • Patient advocate — Many hospitals have advocates who help with billing.
  • Local legal aid — Free or low-cost legal help for healthcare-related issues.
  • Your provider's billing office — They often handle appeals on your behalf.
